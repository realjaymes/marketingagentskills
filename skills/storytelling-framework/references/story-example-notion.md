# Story Example: Notion

PLG productivity SaaS, freemium plus per-seat, prosumer/team hybrid. Simple-to-considered complexity (an individual can start alone; a team decision happens once real content exists). Numbers marked illustrative are placeholders; do not treat them as real.

## Story Bible

### 0. Story Objective & Calibration

**Objective**: get a small team or individual to start a workspace and bring in real content, not just create an account and leave.

**Calibration**: B2C/prosumer leaning B2B team, Simple-to-Considered complexity.

**Output destination**: Positioning & Website Narrative (Mode A), for the homepage.

### 1. The Hero

A small startup team (3-10 people) or an individual knowledge worker whose docs, tasks, and notes are scattered across a docs app, a task app, and Slack threads.

### 2. The World As It Is

Onboarding a new hire means sending five different links to five different tools, none of which talk to each other. Nobody can find the "current" version of anything, so questions get asked in Slack that are already answered somewhere, just not findable.

### 3. The Want

Practical: one connected place for docs, tasks, and knowledge that doesn't require a tool switch every ten minutes. Identity: to run a team that feels organized and legible, not one held together by tribal knowledge and Slack scroll-back.

### 4. The Problem

- **External** — docs, tasks, and notes live in disconnected tools with no shared structure, so information gets duplicated, lost, or trapped in one person's head.
- **Internal** — quiet embarrassment when a new hire asks "where's X documented" and the honest answer is "ask Dave."
- **Philosophical** — a team shouldn't need five subscriptions and constant context-switching just to track its own knowledge. That complexity is imposed by the tools, not the work.

### 5. The False Path

A patchwork of point tools (a docs app, a task app, a wiki, a spreadsheet), each fine alone, none connected, with someone quietly becoming the "human search engine" for where things live.

### 6. The Guide

**Empathy**: leads with "tools should adapt to how you work, not the other way around," speaking directly to teams frustrated by rigid point tools.

**Authority**: a flexible database-and-page model used from solo founders through large enterprises, a deep template ecosystem, and strong word-of-mouth adoption.

### 7. The Plan

Start from a template or a blank page, bring in docs and tasks as you go, invite the team once the workspace has real content in it (not before, so it's not empty and unconvincing).

### 8. The Stakes

| Cost of inaction | Reward of action |
|---|---|
| Knowledge keeps living in individual heads and Slack threads; onboarding stays slow and repetitive; the team keeps paying for and switching between disconnected tools | One searchable home for docs and tasks; faster onboarding; less "ask Dave" |

### 9. The Call to Action

**Direct**: create a free workspace.

**Transitional**: browse the template gallery to see what a workspace could look like for this specific use case.

### 10. World As It Could Be

A new hire's first day includes one link, and every question they'd normally ask in Slack is already answered in a page they can find themselves within the first hour.

## Applied: Positioning & Website Narrative (Mode A)

**Hero section** (Parts 2-4, compressed): "Docs in one tool, tasks in another, and the 'real' answer living in someone's head. One connected workspace for your team's docs, tasks, and knowledge."

**Subhead** (Part 5, implied not named): "Stop being five tools stitched together with Slack messages."

**Feature section** (Part 7, process plan only): "Start from a template → bring in your content → invite the team."

**CTA band** (Part 9): Direct CTA ("Get Notion free") as the primary button, transitional CTA ("Browse templates") as a secondary link.
