# Example: PostHog vs. Mixpanel/Amplitude

Unified-platform versus point-tool dynamics. Figures marked _illustrative_ are placeholders.

## 1. Intelligence Excerpt

| Category | Finding | Source | Confidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| Product | Mixpanel/Amplitude are best-in-class dedicated analytics tools; replay, flags, and experiments require separate vendors | Public product comparison | Verified |
| Product | PostHog bundles analytics, replay, flags, and experiments on one event schema, open-source and self-hostable | Public product comparison | Verified |
| Market | Mixpanel/Amplitude have longer market presence and deeper enterprise mindshare | Public market knowledge | Verified |

## 2. SWOT Synthesis

- **Strength (theirs)**: longer track record, enterprise mindshare, mature pure-play analytics depth.
- **Weakness (theirs)**: no native replay or feature-flag capability — teams still run 2-3 separate vendor contracts and reconcile data manually → maps to Key Differentiators and Why We Win.
- **Opportunity (ours)**: engineering-led teams frustrated by tool sprawl and per-event cost growth are the clearest displacement target.
- **Threat**: if a prospect's committee has an established vendor relationship with procurement/security already cleared, switching cost can override the unified-platform argument.

## 3. Battlecard

**Overview**: PostHog is a unified, open-source product platform — analytics, replay, feature flags, and experiments on one event schema, self-hostable or cloud.

**Mixpanel/Amplitude, in one line**: mature, dedicated product analytics tools requiring separate vendors for replay and feature flags.

**Key differentiators** (illustrative):

| | PostHog | Mixpanel/Amplitude |
|---|---|---|
| Unified platform | ★★★★★ | ★★☆☆☆ |
| Pure analytics depth | ★★★★☆ | ★★★★★ |
| Self-host option | ★★★★★ | ★☆☆☆☆ |

**Why we win**: one event schema instead of three reconciled manually `[proof needed]`; self-hostable for data-residency/security requirements `[proof needed]`.

**Handling objections**: *"We need enterprise-grade analytics depth, not a bundle."* → concede where true for analytics-only power users; redirect to the specific team's usage pattern (do they use replay/flags elsewhere already?).

**Questions to ask**: "How many analytics-adjacent tools are you paying for today, and do they share data with each other?"

## 4. Win-Loss Validation (illustrative, lost deal)

**Excerpt**: "We already had an enterprise contract with our analytics vendor... it came down to contract timing more than product. Our contract still had 8 months left, and nobody wanted to eat that cost to switch early."

**Coded**: primary loss reason = Timing (existing contract term), not product or price.

**Synthesis (illustrative 3-interview sample)**: 2 of 3 cite contract timing as decisive; 1 of 3 cites competitor advantage (deeper historical data retention). Confidence: Medium.

**Feeds**: "when does your current analytics contract renew" should become a standard discovery question — deals lost to timing are recoverable later if tracked, unlike deals lost to product gaps. Add a contract-renewal-timing item to Questions to Ask.
