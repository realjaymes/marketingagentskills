# Example: Notion vs. Confluence

Prosumer flexibility versus enterprise-structured wiki, a widely-discussed public comparison. Figures marked _illustrative_ are placeholders.

## 1. Intelligence Excerpt

| Category | Finding | Source | Confidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| Market | Confluence is deeply embedded in Atlassian-stack shops (paired with Jira) | Public market knowledge | Verified |
| Product | Confluence's page structure is more rigid/hierarchical; Notion's block model is more flexible | Public product comparison | Verified |
| Positioning | Confluence positions on enterprise admin controls and Jira integration; Notion on flexibility and setup speed | Public site comparison | Verified |
| Sales | Confluence often sells as part of an Atlassian bundle, not standalone | Public market knowledge | Verified |

## 2. SWOT Synthesis

- **Strength (theirs)**: default choice for teams already committed to the Atlassian ecosystem — switching cost is the moat, not product quality.
- **Weakness (theirs)**: rigid hierarchy and slower setup for teams without a decided information architecture → maps to Key Differentiators.
- **Opportunity (ours)**: teams that outgrew shared-drive-and-Slack but aren't Atlassian-committed are the clearest win → maps to Customer Pain Points.
- **Threat**: if the buying committee includes an engineering lead already bought into Jira, the bundle argument can override product quality → flag in Questions to Ask.

## 3. Battlecard

**Overview**: Notion is a flexible workspace combining docs, tasks, and wikis in one connected tool.

**Confluence, in one line**: Atlassian's enterprise wiki, most often adopted as part of a broader Jira/Atlassian toolchain.

**Key differentiators** (illustrative):

| | Notion | Confluence |
|---|---|---|
| Setup speed | ★★★★★ | ★★★☆☆ |
| Flexibility | ★★★★★ | ★★★☆☆ |
| Jira-ecosystem integration | ★★☆☆☆ | ★★★★★ |

**Why we win**: faster time to a usable workspace `[proof needed]`; one tool instead of docs-plus-wiki-plus-tasks `[proof needed]`.

**Handling objections**: *"We already use Jira, so Confluence is the natural fit."* → "That's real to weigh. Ask whether the actual complaint is about Jira, or about docs and knowledge living somewhere separate from where the team works day to day."

**Questions to ask**: "Is Jira actually driving daily complaints, or is it the docs/knowledge side that's painful?"

## 4. Win-Loss Validation (illustrative, won deal)

**Excerpt**: "We evaluated Confluence because of our Jira setup. It felt heavier to set up and our first attempt at structuring it stalled out... what pushed us over was how fast we could get something usable."

**Coded**: primary win reason = Product fit / setup speed, not a feature checklist win.

**Synthesis (illustrative 3-interview sample)**: 3 of 3 cite setup speed as decisive; 2 of 3 raised the Jira-integration objection but it wasn't decisive. Confidence: High.

**Feeds**: confirms the battlecard's Why We Win claim on setup speed — replace `[proof needed]` with this finding once a real sample backs it. The Jira objection is real and recurring even when not decisive — keep it staffed with a strong response rather than treating it as rare.
